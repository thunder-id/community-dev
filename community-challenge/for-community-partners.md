# Host a ThunderID Community Challenge

ThunderID Community Challenges help developers learn about a modern identity topic, build working solutions with [ThunderID](https://thunderid.dev), and share useful knowledge with the wider open-source community.

A challenge may be physical, virtual, or hybrid. The usual format combines a community-hosted kickoff, a two-week build period, technical support, public project submissions, and joint evaluation.

Possible challenge topics include:

- Building secure agentic applications.
- Integrating applications with digital wallets.
- Creating decentralized identity experiences.
- Deploying ThunderID in cloud-native environments.
- Integrating ThunderID with application frameworks, developer tools, and open-source platforms.

## Why host a challenge?

Hosting a challenge can help your community:

- Give members practical knowledge of an emerging identity topic.
- Connect participants with ThunderID developers and maintainers.
- Help members create public projects that demonstrate applied knowledge.
- Encourage useful open-source collaboration and knowledge-sharing.
- Offer sponsored prizes and community recognition.
- Join a growing network of communities helping developers learn, build, and share.

## Programme structure

1. **Agree on the challenge:** The host community, the ThunderID team, and the sponsor agree on the topic, prizes, dates, venue, delivery format, and available support. This can be handled through email, a short call, a chat, or whatever is most convenient—there is no formal planning process to follow.
2. **Promote the challenge:** The host community and the ThunderID team announce the challenge through their existing channels. The same approach you already use to promote meetups and community events will usually work best; no separate marketing campaign is expected.
3. **Run the kickoff:** The host community opens and facilitates a kickoff session, which can be run as one of its regular meetups.
4. **Build for two weeks:** Registered participants usually have two weeks to develop their projects, use the support channel, and attend optional office hours. Community involvement during this period can be minimal and is optional; participants can engage directly with the available support channels.
5. **Evaluate and announce:** An evaluator from the ThunderID team and an evaluator nominated by the host community assess the submissions, shortlist the winners, and announce the results jointly. An independent third evaluator may join when available.

## What the community partner provides

- **Nominate a coordinator and evaluator:** A senior community member coordinates with the ThunderID team and the sponsor. The coordinator and evaluator may be the same person, but anyone serving as an evaluator cannot participate as a competitor in that challenge.
- **Promote the challenge:** Use the same channels and methods you already use for meetups and community events. Nothing additional or specialized is required.
- **Open and facilitate the kickoff:** Run the kickoff in the same way you would run a normal community event. It is usually around two hours and may be physical, hybrid, or completely virtual, depending on what works for your community.
- **Help evaluate the submissions:** The community's nominated evaluator works with the evaluator from the ThunderID team to assess submissions, shortlist winners, and confirm the results.

## Recommended kickoff format

There is no mandatory format. A typical two-hour kickoff includes:

1. **Community welcome and challenge introduction:** A community organizer explains the challenge, programme structure, important dates, and how members can participate.
2. **Technical introduction:** A developer or maintainer from the ThunderID team joins remotely for a 30-to-45-minute session, followed by questions and answers. Its purpose is to give participants enough knowledge about ThunderID and the challenge topic to start building; no prior product expertise is expected.
3. **Registration:** Registration is opened or explained after the technical session. The host community may use its own registration system or a simple form such as Google Forms. The ThunderID team can help provide the registration process when required.

There is no minimum number of participants and no expectation that a community recruit hundreds of people. A group similar in size to one of your regular meetups is appropriate. The emphasis is on participants who genuinely want to learn and build.

## What each participant should do

1. Attend the kickoff and register for the challenge, either individually or as part of a small team.
2. Use the usual two-week build period to create a working project with ThunderID. Participants can use office hours and the community support channel to ask questions and discuss their work.
3. Submit the three required deliverables before the deadline.
4. Demonstrate the project and contribute useful knowledge back to the community.

## Participant deliverables

Each participant or team normally provides three deliverables:

1. **Public code repository:** Source code, an appropriate open-source licence, setup instructions, and an explanation of how ThunderID is used.
2. **Project demonstration:** A working application accessible online where practical, a live demonstration, or a recorded demonstration video.
3. **Community contribution:** A useful explanation of the project and lessons learned. This may be a blog post, video, technical walkthrough, architecture explanation, social-media content, or another appropriate format.

## Evaluation

The evaluation panel normally consists of:

- One evaluator from the ThunderID team.
- One evaluator nominated by the host community.
- When available, one independent third-party evaluator.

| Evaluation area | Points |
|---|---:|
| Meaningful use of ThunderID and challenge concepts | 30 |
| Technical implementation and completeness | 25 |
| Practical usefulness and potential impact | 20 |
| Originality | 15 |
| Community contribution | 10 |
| **Total** | **100** |

Community contribution is judged by the quality, effort, and usefulness of what participants share, including constructive engagement with the community. It is not judged by the number of posts, followers, views, likes, or reactions.

## Typical prizes

The prize package is agreed among the host community, sponsor, and programme team for each challenge. A typical package is:

- **First place:** Around USD 1,500
- **Second place:** Around USD 1000
- **Third place:** Around USD 500
- **Best Community Voice:** Around USD 250

Exact amounts, eligibility, and distribution arrangements are confirmed before the challenge is announced.

## What the programme provides

- Challenge structure and topic-specific guidance.
- A ThunderID technical presenter for the kickoff.
- Participant resources and suggested project areas.
- Remote technical support and optional office hours.
- Help with registration when required.
- Evaluation guidance and scoring materials.
- Sponsored participant prizes.
- Agreed support for physical, virtual, or hybrid delivery.

The exact support will be agreed with each community based on its proposed format and requirements.

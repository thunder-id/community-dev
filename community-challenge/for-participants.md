# Participate in a ThunderID Community Challenge

ThunderID Community Challenges give developers an opportunity to learn about an emerging identity topic, build a working project with [ThunderID](https://thunderid.dev), share their knowledge, and compete for prizes through an open-source community.

## What you can gain

- Practical knowledge of a modern identity problem.
- Hands-on experience building with ThunderID.
- Guidance from ThunderID developers and maintainers.
- A public project, community recognition, and the opportunity to win prizes.

Each challenge publishes its specific topic, learning outcomes, technical resources, and prize package.

## Typical prizes

- **First place:** Around USD 1,500
- **Second place:** Around USD 1000
- **Third place:** Around USD 500
- **Best Community Voice:** Around USD 250

Exact prizes, eligibility requirements, and payment arrangements are published with each challenge.

## How to participate

1. **Find a challenge:** Select an active challenge hosted by a participating open-source community.
2. **Attend the kickoff:** Learn about the challenge topic, programme structure, relevant ThunderID capabilities, available support, and important dates.
3. **Register your project:** Register individually or as a small team with a project title, short description, and intended use of ThunderID. Registration is not an approval gate, and you may refine your idea during the challenge.
4. **Build for two weeks:** Develop, test, and document your project during the usual two-week build period.
5. **Submit and demonstrate:** Provide the required public deliverables and demonstrate the completed project.
6. **Share what you learned:** Contribute useful knowledge back to the community through an appropriate public format.

## Find a challenge

Individuals can participate only through a challenge listed on the current-challenges page and hosted by a participating community.

[View current challenges](challenges.md)

If no suitable challenge is available, ask an open-source community you participate in to host one, or work with an existing community to propose and organize a challenge.

## What to expect at the kickoff

The community-hosted kickoff is usually around two hours. The exact format may vary, but it generally includes:

- A welcome and challenge introduction from the host community.
- A 30-to-45-minute technical introduction from a ThunderID developer or maintainer, designed to give you enough knowledge to start building.
- Questions and answers.
- Registration guidance, important dates, and submission requirements.

## During the two-week build period

Participants are expected to develop a working project, test the main use case, and prepare the required public materials.

Support may include:

- Scheduled technical office hours.
- A community support channel or mailing list.
- Technical resources and example projects.
- Answers from maintainers, organizers, and other participants.

Participants are encouraged to ask useful questions, discuss their approach, and help others when possible. Constructive community engagement may contribute to the community-contribution score, but message volume and social popularity do not.

## How to get help

If you have a technical question about ThunderID, use one of these community channels:

1. **Discord:** Ask the ThunderID community for help. [Join the Discord channel](INSERT_THUNDERID_DISCORD_URL).
2. **GitHub Discussions:** Ask questions, discuss implementation approaches, and learn from other community members in [ThunderID GitHub Discussions](https://github.com/thunder-id/thunderid/discussions).
3. **GitHub Issues:** If you find a reproducible product defect or want to request an improvement, search for an existing report or [open a GitHub issue](https://github.com/thunder-id/thunderid/issues).

## Required deliverables

Each participant or team normally provides three deliverables:

1. **Public code repository:** Source code, an appropriate open-source licence, setup instructions, and an explanation of how ThunderID is used.
2. **Project demonstration:** A working application accessible online where practical, a live demonstration, or a recorded demonstration video.
3. **Community contribution:** A blog post, video, technical walkthrough, architecture explanation, social-media content, or another useful public explanation of the project and lessons learned.

Participants may use AI-assisted development tools, but remain responsible for understanding, testing, documenting, licensing, and explaining all submitted work.

## How projects are evaluated

The panel normally includes one evaluator from the ThunderID team, one evaluator nominated by the host community, and, when available, an independent third-party evaluator.

| Evaluation area | Points |
|---|---:|
| Meaningful use of ThunderID and challenge concepts | 30 |
| Technical implementation and completeness | 25 |
| Practical usefulness and potential impact | 20 |
| Originality | 15 |
| Community contribution | 10 |
| **Total** | **100** |

Community contribution is evaluated by the quality, effort, and usefulness of the participant's knowledge-sharing and constructive engagement—not by posts, followers, views, likes, or reactions.

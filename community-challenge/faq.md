# Frequently Asked Questions

## What is the ThunderID Community Challenge?

It is a community-led programme where developers learn about a modern identity topic, build a working solution with [ThunderID](https://thunderid.dev), and share what they learned with the wider open-source community.

## Who can participate?

Each published challenge defines its own eligibility requirements. The programme is primarily designed for professional developers, architects, platform engineers, security practitioners, and open-source contributors. Students may participate when permitted by the specific challenge.

Always read the eligibility requirements before registering. Challenges may be limited to particular countries or regions because prizes are processed regionally by the sponsor. Registering does not override a challenge's location, age, employment, legal, or prize-processing requirements.

## Can I participate independently?

No. You cannot apply to the overall programme or submit a project independently of a published community challenge.

You may participate as an individual developer, but you must register for and take part through an active challenge hosted by a participating community. Before registering, confirm that you satisfy that challenge's participant and regional eligibility requirements.

[View current challenges](challenges.md)

## Can teams participate?

Yes, when team participation is permitted by the specific challenge. Each team must nominate one member as its primary contact. The programme will communicate with that person and, if the team wins, the sponsor will process the prize through the nominated contact, subject to the applicable terms and compliance checks.

The team is responsible for managing its membership, deciding how work is organized, and distributing any prize among its members. The programme, host community, evaluators, and sponsor are not responsible for internal team arrangements or prize-sharing disputes.

## What if there is no challenge available for me?

You can ask an open-source community you belong to to host a challenge, or work with an existing community to propose one.

[Learn how to host a challenge](for-community-partners.md)

## Can an individual host a challenge?

An individual may propose and coordinate a challenge, but it must be operated through an appropriate community that can promote and facilitate the programme and nominate an evaluator.

## Are challenges physical or online?

A challenge may be physical, virtual, or hybrid. The format is selected with the host community and published on the individual challenge page.

## How long does a challenge run?

The build period is usually two weeks. Each challenge publishes its own kickoff, registration, office-hours, submission, and evaluation dates.

## Does it cost anything to participate?

The programme does not normally charge a participation fee. Any local arrangements or exceptions will be stated on the individual challenge page before registration.

## What topics do the challenges cover?

Topics may include securing agentic applications, digital-wallet integration, decentralized identity, cloud-native deployment, developer-framework integrations, post-quantum-ready identity, and securing modern applications or workloads.

## What must I submit?

Submissions normally include a public source repository, a project demonstration, and a public community contribution explaining the project and lessons learned. Each challenge publishes its detailed requirements.

## Can I use AI development tools?

Yes. AI-assisted software development is allowed, and you may use AI tools as part of your normal development workflow.

However, the challenge is intended to help developers explore a demanding identity problem, gain hands-on experience with ThunderID, and contribute useful knowledge to the community. Submitting an application generated end-to-end from the challenge description, without meaningful personal involvement or understanding, does not meet that objective.

You must:

- Make substantive technical and design decisions.
- Work with and understand the relevant ThunderID capabilities.
- Test and verify the submitted solution.
- Understand and comply with the licences that apply to generated and reused material.
- Be able to explain the architecture, code, security decisions, limitations, and lessons learned.
- Remain accountable for everything you submit.

Evaluators may ask you to explain, demonstrate, troubleshoot, or make a small change to your project. If they have reasonable grounds to conclude that you do not understand the submitted work, did not meaningfully engage with the challenge, or cannot take responsibility for the result, they may exclude the submission from evaluation.

The simplest rule is: use AI tools as you would in responsible professional development. AI may assist your work, but it cannot replace your participation, judgment, or understanding.

## How are projects evaluated?

Projects are generally assessed on meaningful ThunderID usage, technical implementation, practical usefulness, originality, and community contribution. Each challenge publishes its evaluation criteria.

## How is community contribution evaluated?

Community contribution is evaluated by quality, effort, usefulness, and constructive engagement—not by the number of posts, followers, views, likes, or reactions.

## What prizes are available?

A typical prize package is around USD 1,500 for first place, USD 1000 for second place, USD 500 for third place, and USD 250 for Best Community Voice. Exact amounts and conditions are published for each challenge.

## Who provides technical support?

ThunderID developers and maintainers provide the technical learning session, participant resources, remote guidance, and optional office hours agreed for each challenge.

## How can my community host a challenge?

Review the community-partner guidance and submit an expression of interest. A team member will contact you to discuss the topic, format, support, prizes, and schedule.

## Why this challenge matters

Most agent development focuses on models, tools, orchestration, and MCP connections. Identity is often addressed later, even though an agent may access data, call APIs, use tools, or act on behalf of a person or organization.

When an agentic application moves toward production, developers must answer important questions:

- Who is the agent?
- Whom does it represent?
- What is it allowed to access?
- How is authority delegated, limited, and revoked?
- How can its actions be traced and audited?

This challenge gives you an opportunity to explore those questions by building a working agentic application that uses ThunderID meaningfully.

## What you can gain

By participating, you can:

- Learn why AI agents need first-class identities.
- Gain hands-on experience with delegated authority and least-privilege access.
- Explore consent, approval, expiration, and revocation for agent actions.
- Learn how to protect the APIs, tools, and resources used by agents.
- Make agent actions traceable and auditable.
- Receive guidance from ThunderID developers and maintainers.
- Create a public project that demonstrates your knowledge.
- Share your experience and contribute useful knowledge to the open-source community.

You may work individually or as part of a small team. Your project should demonstrate one or more agent-identity concepts through a working implementation.
